Make defect report a fast & convenient experience for citizen via Smart City App

Designing a feature that enables faster, easier reporting of city issues, and boosts engagement in a real Smart City app.

Role UX UI Design
Employer make-better
Date 2023

Context

Mein Duisburg is a mobile app made for citizens of Duisburg city in Germany. It's a "super" app that offers everything from what's happening in the city, run for the environment – Klimagold, checking out garbage collection schedule, finding charging stations to even report a defect to the city.

The defect reporting module allows residents to flag infrastructure issues — potholes, broken street lights, damaged benches — directly to the responsible municipal department, without needing to call a hotline or fill out a paper form.

Goal

The objective of this feature was to help residents report problems directly while on the go, without having to fill out complicated forms. The system automatically forwards reports to the responsible teams at the same time, increasing citizen participation by making the feature intuitive and trustworthy.

<60s Target submission time
0 Manual routing steps for citizens
2 wks Average city resolution time

Research

To kickstart the design process, I discussed with our Product Owner to learn more about the user pain points which are:

  • No central place to report public defects but instead manual reports via email or phone
  • Reporting process felt time-consuming and unclear
  • Lack of feedback loop after a report is submitted
  • Most of these problems are processed and resolved by the responsible authorities within two weeks

I also reviewed competing civic apps and similar reporting flows (BürgerApp, FixMyStreet) to understand patterns citizens were already familiar with.

"People want to report a problem in under a minute. If they have to think too long, they give up and the issue never gets fixed."

Problem

The existing reporting path required citizens to find the right department's contact, send an email with a description, and manually attach photos — with no confirmation that anything would happen. This created a high drop-off rate and low trust in the system.

Key design challenges

  • Reduce cognitive load — citizens should not need to know which department handles which defect type
  • Make location input frictionless — people are often on the go when they spot an issue
  • Build trust through clear status feedback — what happens after I submit?
  • Handle AI-generated content responsibly — the system uses AI to pre-fill category and description from photos

Approach

Progressive disclosure with a step-by-step flow

Rather than presenting a long form up front, I broke the report into four focused steps: add photos → confirm details → add address → review & send. This reduced the perceived effort and kept each screen to a single decision.

AI-assisted categorisation

The app uses AI to automatically generate a suggested category and description from the uploaded photos. Citizens see a transparent notice that the content was AI-generated and can edit it before submitting. This keeps things fast while preserving user control.

Smart location input

The address step defaults to the user's current GPS location with a map confirmation. Citizens can tap to adjust the pin if the GPS is slightly off — a common real-world scenario when reporting a nearby issue.

Clear feedback states

Every submission returns a confirmation with a unique report ID and an explanation of the next steps. Push notifications inform the user when the city team receives and acts on the report.

Process

I started with low-fidelity sketches to validate the step-by-step flow with the product team. After aligning on the structure, I moved into mid-fidelity wireframes and ran a quick usability session with five citizens to catch friction points before going into high fidelity.

Key insights from usability testing

  • Users expected to be able to skip the photo step for defects that are hard to photograph (e.g. noise complaints) — we added an optional path
  • The AI-generated description confused two participants who thought the app was "putting words in my mouth" — we added a clearer "AI-assisted, you can edit" label
  • The progress indicator at the top was not visible enough on the photo step — we increased contrast and added step labels

Final Design

The final screens deliver a clean, four-step reporting flow that stays true to the Mein Duisburg design system while introducing clear, accessible components for photo upload, address confirmation, and AI content disclosure.

Step 1 — Add photos

Citizens can upload up to three photos with guidance from a visual image guide. Photos are optional but recommended to help the AI generate an accurate category.

Step 2 — Confirm details

The AI-generated category and description are shown with a clear disclaimer. Citizens can accept as-is or tap to edit before continuing.

Step 3 — Add address

A map centred on the user's GPS location lets them confirm or adjust the exact spot. Street address is also shown as a text fallback.

Step 4 — Review & send

A summary of the full report before submission, with a final CTA. A confirmation screen with report ID follows immediately after.

Outcome

The redesigned defect report feature shipped as part of the Mein Duisburg app update in 2023. The step-by-step flow with AI assistance significantly reduced the time required to file a report and lowered the barrier for first-time users.

  • Average report submission time reduced to under 60 seconds in internal testing
  • AI-assisted categorisation correctly pre-filled the defect category in approximately 80% of test cases
  • Positive feedback from the product team and early users on clarity of the feedback states
  • The design pattern was later reused as a template for additional reporting flows in the app

The project reinforced the value of progressive disclosure and transparent AI interactions — two principles I now apply as defaults in any form-heavy flow.
